The internet that once felt like a sprawling public square is now a landscape of noise, monetized feeds, and AI‑driven filler. A long‑time community builder describes how the shift from thoughtful discussion to attention‑grabbing content has drained his enjoyment. He points to the rise of short‑form platforms and the flood of machine‑written articles as key symptoms. For developers who rely on the web for learning, networking, and inspiration, this trend threatens the quality of the resources they depend on. Understanding why the medium is changing helps us decide how to adapt our own workflows.

<Callout type="takeaway">The internet’s value is eroding as monetization and AI‑driven content crowd out genuine knowledge sharing.</Callout>

## What happened
The author grew up on message boards, blogs, and early social networks, crediting them with friendships, jobs, and core beliefs. Over the years he observed a steady decline as platforms prioritized ad revenue, replacing nuanced discussion with click‑bait and low‑effort posts. The recent surge of large language model‑generated articles has amplified the problem, making it hard to distinguish original thought from filler.

He likens the spread of AI‑generated noise to the “pale” in Disco Elysium, a corrosive force that turns once‑vibrant islands of insight into barren wasteland. Short‑form feeds on Instagram, TikTok, X, and even newer services like Bluesky feel increasingly tribal and intellectually lazy. Even longer‑form venues such as Hacker News now surface many posts that read like generic LLM output, further diminishing the incentive to read or write.

## Why it matters
Developers and builders use the web as a primary learning pipeline, from tutorials to design debates. When signal‑to‑noise ratios drop, discovery becomes costly, leading to wasted time and missed opportunities. Communities that once self‑policed quality are losing that guard, so misinformation and shallow trends can spread unchecked, influencing product decisions and career paths. Moreover, the erosion of thoughtful discourse reduces the chance for interdisciplinary cross‑pollination that fuels innovation.

## How to think about it
Treat the internet as a layered resource: use curated newsletters or community‑run digests for depth, reserve short‑form feeds for quick inspiration, and apply a verification step before trusting any LLM‑generated article. Build personal filters—RSS, quality tags, or AI‑assisted summarizers that highlight original citations. When you encounter a post that feels generic, check the author’s history or look for external references. Consider contributing high‑quality content to maintain the islands you still value.

<Callout type="warning">Don’t dismiss every AI‑generated piece; some provide useful scaffolding, but always verify the underlying sources.</Callout>
