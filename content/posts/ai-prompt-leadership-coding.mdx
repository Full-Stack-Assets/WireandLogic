When I first started treating large language models like a compiler, the results were erratic and frustrating. I quickly realized that getting consistent output required the same kind of context‑setting, clarification, and feedback I use when leading a team. The AI would reinterpret the same prompt in different ways, surfacing hidden assumptions and prompting me to explain the why behind the request. Understanding this shift is crucial for developers who want to harness AI effectively without falling back on brittle, instruction‑only code.

<Callout type="takeaway">Working with AI is a leadership exercise, not just a coding task.</Callout>

## What happened
AI runs on software, but its behavior is not fully deterministic. The same request can yield a useful connection, miss an obvious point, or surprise me with an unexpected approach. This unpredictability makes context, clarity, and feedback more valuable than a simple command.

Good leaders share context, explain the desired outcome, set boundaries, and respond to what comes back. Applying those habits to AI—providing a shared working context, examples, corrections, and reusable instructions—reduces misunderstandings and aligns the system with how I think.

## Why it matters
If developers continue to treat AI as a deterministic compiler, they will spend more time fighting flaky outputs than building value. By adopting a leadership mindset, teams can improve alignment, lower debugging overhead, and create prompts that scale across projects. The shift also broadens the skill set of engineers, making them better communicators and collaborators.

## How to think about it
Start each interaction by stating the broader goal, not just the immediate task. Follow with any constraints, examples of desired output, and a brief explanation of why the result matters. Treat the AI’s reply as a draft—provide corrections, ask follow‑up questions, and iterate until the response matches the intent. Over time, build a library of reusable instructions that capture recurring patterns, just as a leader refines playbooks for their team.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the AI has human judgment or accountability; always validate critical decisions yourself.</Callout>
