Developers are voicing a growing dislike for AI‑generated images in technical blogs. A recent personal essay and the ensuing Hacker News discussion highlight how such visuals can make readers question the authenticity of the writing itself. For indie developers, credibility is a key currency, and visual choices now directly affect that perception. This post examines the backlash, its implications, and practical steps you can take.

<Callout type="takeaway">AI‑generated images can erode reader trust and reduce engagement with technical blogs.</Callout>

## What happened
The author of a personal blog expressed a strong aversion to AI‑generated images, arguing that their presence makes readers suspect the text is also AI‑written. The same sentiment echoed on Hacker News, where commenters suggested that obvious AI art signals low effort and can distract from the article’s substance. A third source offered a decision framework, noting that while AI art is cheap and fast, it often looks artificial and may provoke doubts about the author’s expertise.

## Why it matters
Credibility is essential for developers sharing knowledge or promoting open‑source projects. When readers see a generic AI‑generated illustration, they may infer that the author cut corners, leading to lower engagement, fewer shares, and reduced trust in future content. For independent bloggers, this perception can directly impact audience growth and professional reputation.

## How to think about it
1. **Assess audience expectations** – Technical readers value transparency; if authenticity matters, favor hand‑crafted or curated stock images.
2. **Use AI as a tool, not a crutch** – Generate placeholders quickly, then replace them with vetted illustrations or simple diagrams you create yourself.
3. **Make the source clear** – If you do use AI art, add a brief credit or note to avoid the “is this AI?” speculation.
4. **Test before publishing** – Share a draft with a trusted peer; if they comment on the image’s credibility, reconsider.
5. **Prioritize function over flair** – For posts about code snippets or architecture diagrams, a clean schematic often serves better than a stylized AI picture.

<Callout type="warning">Avoid relying on AI images as the sole visual cue for credibility; they can backfire if readers perceive them as lazy or deceptive.</Callout>
