A 62.5 g Mars bar stamped 1991 turned up in a Scunthorpe house clearance, instantly going viral. The bar is almost a hand‑length long, dwarfing the current 40 g version by about 20 g. The find has reignited the conversation around “shrinkflation,” where manufacturers reduce size while keeping price steady. For developers and product teams, the episode is a reminder that subtle changes can have outsized public impact.

<Callout type="takeaway">Mars bars have shrunk by roughly a third, a tangible example of shrinkflation in everyday products.</Callout>

## What happened
During a clear‑out of a hoarder’s house in Scunthorpe, cleaning‑service owner Victoria Gordon posted a photo of a 62.5 g Mars bar next to a modern 40 g bar. The older bar’s best‑before date reads 1991, making it about 35 years old.

Gordon told BBC Radio Lincolnshire, "It was nearly as big as my hand, which was kind of why I noticed it." A Mars spokesperson added, "Over the last 35 years, we have made a number of updates to our bar sizes and pack formats to reflect consumer demand, alongside considering wider external factors such as manufacturing costs and the price of cocoa."

## Why it matters
The size reduction illustrates shrinkflation, a tactic where companies keep prices stable while quietly decreasing product volume. For consumers, the hidden loss erodes purchasing power and can damage brand trust when the change becomes public. For product teams, the episode shows how incremental adjustments—whether in UI dimensions, API payloads, or physical goods—can generate backlash if not communicated transparently.

## How to think about it
Treat any reduction in a product’s outward‑facing metric—size, bandwidth, or feature count—as a version change that requires clear documentation and user communication. Track the metric in your telemetry, set alerts for unexpected drifts, and consider a changelog entry or UI badge when you adjust it. Transparency helps maintain trust and gives users a chance to adapt or provide feedback before the change becomes a public controversy.
