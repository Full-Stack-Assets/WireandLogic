Denmark’s Ministry of Education announced an emergency measure that forces upper‑secondary students to verbally defend any written assignment completed at home. The rule, effective immediately, targets the roughly 9,000 pupils in the two‑year Higher Preparatory Examination program. Officials say the step is needed to stop a surge in AI‑generated cheating. By requiring an oral explanation, the government hopes to verify genuine understanding and preserve academic integrity. The move also bundles new screen‑monitoring tools and firewalls for in‑class exams.

<Callout type="takeaway">Oral defenses are Denmark’s swift answer to AI‑driven academic dishonesty.</Callout>

## What happened
The ministry’s statement outlined three initiatives: a mandatory oral defense for all home‑written exams, the deployment of screen‑monitoring software during supervised tests, and a firewall to restrict AI‑accessible content. Education Minister Magnus Heunicke warned that AI misuse threatens students’ skills and critical thinking, and pledged ongoing dialogue with schools, teachers, and students.

School organisations welcomed the rapid response but urged longer‑term solutions, noting the rapid pace of AI development. Teachers’ union leader Anders Frikke suggested additional measures such as mandatory AI‑use disclosures and AI‑free preparation periods for oral exams.

## Why it matters
If successful, the policy could set a precedent for other nations grappling with AI‑assisted plagiarism, shifting assessment culture toward more interactive verification. However, it also raises questions about student workload, privacy, and whether oral exams can truly detect sophisticated language models. The balance between deterrence and over‑regulation will shape future education technology strategies.

## How to think about it
Educators should treat the oral defense as a supplement, not a replacement, and design concise questioning that probes reasoning rather than rote recall. Ed‑tech builders can support this shift by offering secure video‑assessment platforms, automated scheduling, and analytics that flag inconsistencies between written and spoken responses. At the same time, policies must include safeguards for student privacy and mental health, ensuring that monitoring tools are transparent and proportionate.

<Callout type="warning">Do not rely solely on oral defenses to catch AI cheating; combine with robust plagiarism detection and clear AI‑use policies.</Callout>
