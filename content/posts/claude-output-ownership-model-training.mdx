Claude’s outputs belong to the user, but Anthropic’s terms explicitly forbid using those outputs to train new AI models without permission. This policy was clarified in a recent support article that outlines permitted and prohibited uses. The restriction aims to protect safety controls and prevent competitors from leveraging Anthropic’s investment. For developers, the rule shapes how they can reuse generated content in downstream projects.

<Callout type="takeaway">Owning Claude’s outputs does not give you permission to train competing models.</Callout>

## What happened
Anthropic states that customers own the outputs generated by Claude, yet the service agreement bars using those outputs to train or develop AI models without written consent. The company cites safety concerns: models trained on Claude’s outputs would lack Anthropic’s safety layers, creating potential for harmful behavior. Additionally, using the service to build competing models would leverage Anthropic’s research and infrastructure to undermine its product offerings.

The policy does allow non‑competitive uses, such as building specialized classifiers, integrating outputs into applications, or improving internal workflows. Any training that aims to produce a model capable of open‑ended text generation and that could compete with Claude is expressly prohibited.

## Why it matters
Developers looking to fine‑tune or distill models must treat Claude’s outputs as off‑limits data for generative training, or risk violating the terms of service. Ignoring the restriction can lead to account suspension, legal exposure, and the loss of Anthropic’s safety guarantees in downstream systems. The rule also underscores a broader industry trend where AI providers separate content ownership from model‑training rights to protect their competitive edge and safety investments.

## How to think about it
Treat Claude’s outputs as proprietary content for downstream consumption, not as training data for new language models. If you need a custom generative model, either obtain explicit written permission from Anthropic or use publicly licensed datasets. For narrow tasks—such as sentiment analysis, entity extraction, or domain‑specific classifiers—leveraging the outputs is permissible and can add value without infringing the policy. Always document the intended use and keep a record of any permissions granted.

<Callout type="warning">Do not train any open‑ended text generation model on Claude’s outputs without explicit written consent from Anthropic.</Callout>
