The Commodity Futures Trading Commission invoked its emergency authority on August 12, ordering KalshiEX to keep its event‑contract platform running in New York. The move came after New York Attorney General Letitia James filed a lawsuit seeking a restraining order and more than $36 billion in damages. By mandating continued operation, the CFTC is asserting federal preemption over state gambling laws. This clash pits a federal derivatives regulator against a state that treats prediction markets as gambling. For developers building on Kalshi, the decision reshapes the regulatory landscape overnight.

<Callout type="takeaway">The CFTC’s emergency order forces Kalshi to stay open in New York, reinforcing federal jurisdiction over prediction markets.</Callout>

## What happened
The CFTC announced that it exercised emergency authority after KalshiEX notified the agency of a market emergency triggered by the New York AG’s complaint. In the July 31 lawsuit, New York sought a temporary restraining order and claimed over $36 billion in damages, alleging that Kalshi’s event contracts violate state gambling statutes.

In its press release, Chairman Michael S. Selig warned that New York was trying to “make event contract derivatives waste away under its iron curtain of state gaming laws.” The commission emphasized its mandate under the Commodity Exchange Act to maintain a uniform national market and to protect price discovery and market resilience.

The CFTC also highlighted ongoing litigation against multiple states that have attempted to regulate derivatives markets, underscoring its broader strategy to preempt a patchwork of state rules.

## Why it matters
For developers, the order guarantees that Kalshi’s APIs and market data remain accessible to New York users, preserving a key user base and revenue stream. It also sets a precedent that the federal regulator can intervene quickly when state actions threaten market continuity. However, the underlying legal dispute remains unresolved, meaning future court rulings could still reshape how prediction‑market contracts are classified and taxed.

The decision signals to other prediction‑market platforms that federal preemption is a viable defense, but it also warns that state attorneys general may continue to pursue parallel enforcement, creating a layered compliance environment.

## How to think about it
Treat the CFTC order as a temporary safeguard, not a permanent shield. Build your integration with flexibility: abstract jurisdiction‑specific logic, maintain audit trails for trades, and keep an eye on evolving guidance from both the CFTC and state regulators. Engage legal counsel early to map out scenarios where a state court might still impose restrictions, and consider diversifying across platforms to mitigate concentration risk.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume state lawsuits are moot; enforcement actions can still target specific contract types or user activities even if the platform remains operational.</Callout>
