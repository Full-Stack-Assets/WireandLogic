A new CNBC Generation Labs survey has captured the mood of America’s youngest adults toward the leaders of the AI industry. More than 1,000 respondents aged 18‑34 were asked which AI executives they trusted to act responsibly, and the answers were uniformly bleak. Over 70% of participants said they “don’t trust” each of nine high‑profile CEOs, with Palantir’s Alex Karp receiving the lowest trust rating at 81% distrust. The poll also found that nearly half of young people expect AI to hurt their careers and that a sizable majority want stricter regulation. These findings signal a generational shift that could reshape talent pipelines, public policy, and corporate strategy in the AI sector.

<Callout type="takeaway">Young adults overwhelmingly distrust AI CEOs, with 70%+ saying they don’t trust each leader.</Callout>

## What happened
The poll, conducted in mid‑2026, sampled over 1,000 U.S. adults between 18 and 34 and asked a series of questions about politics, AI, and the economy. One key question asked respondents to identify which of nine AI‑focused executives they trusted to act responsibly on AI, and the results were consistently negative.

Alex Karp of Palantir recorded the steepest rejection, with 81% of respondents indicating they did not trust him. Trust for other CEOs ranged from 35% for Microsoft’s Satya Nadella (the only one to earn a measurable “trust” share) to 69‑71% distrust for Mark Zuckerberg, Elon Musk, and Sam Altman. Peter Thiel saw 79% distrust.

Beyond personal trust, 45% of the cohort said AI would negatively affect their careers, 40% called for government regulation, and 60% wanted to slow the rapid expansion of data centers, suggesting broader concerns about the technology’s societal impact.

## Why it matters
The stark lack of confidence signals a credibility gap that could hamper recruitment, investor confidence, and public legitimacy for AI firms. Companies may face pressure to adopt stronger ethical frameworks, increase transparency, and engage directly with younger stakeholders to rebuild trust. At the same time, policymakers could interpret the findings as a mandate for tighter oversight, potentially reshaping the regulatory landscape that governs AI development and deployment.

## How to think about it
AI leaders should treat the poll as a warning sign and proactively address the underlying concerns: communicate concrete safety measures, involve diverse voices in governance, and demonstrate tangible benefits for the younger workforce. Building credibility now can mitigate future talent shortages and pre‑empt stricter legislative action.

<Callout type="warning">The poll reflects a single U.S. sample and a specific trust question; it should not be taken as a definitive measure of all stakeholder sentiment.</Callout>
