DeepSeek announced that its flagship V4 Pro model has left preview and is now available as the 0813 build. The GA release appears on OpenRouter and DeepSeek’s own API docs, pointing the deepseek‑v4‑pro endpoint to the new version. V4 Pro is a mixture‑of‑experts system with 1.6 trillion total parameters and about 49 billion active parameters per token. The move matters because it gives developers access to a high‑capacity model with long‑context capabilities at the same pricing terms that were offered during preview.

<Callout type="takeaway">DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 brings a trillion‑parameter MoE LLM to production with the same preview pricing, but its high cost and limited concurrency make it a niche choice for heavy‑reasoning workloads.</Callout>

## What happened
As of August 12 2026 the deepseek‑v4‑pro endpoint on DeepSeek’s official API now resolves to DeepSeek‑V4‑Pro‑0813, ending a four‑month preview that began on April 24 2026. The model card on Hugging Face confirms a 1.6 trillion‑parameter MoE architecture, 49 B active parameters per token, and hybrid attention variants that cut single‑token compute to 27 % and KV‑cache usage to 10 % of the previous V3.2 generation.

Pricing carries over unchanged: $0.435 per million input tokens on a cache miss, $0.003625 per million on a cache hit, and $0.87–$0.93 per million output tokens. Concurrency is limited to 500 requests for the Pro endpoint, with a one‑million‑token context window and a maximum output length of 384 k tokens. Benchmark results released by DeepSeek place V4 Pro Max at 80.6 % on SWE‑bench Verified and ahead of Anthropic’s Claude Opus 4.8 on Terminal Bench 2.1.

## Why it matters
The release gives developers a production‑grade, long‑context LLM that can handle heavyweight reasoning tasks such as code generation or complex planning, positioning DeepSeek as a direct competitor to Anthropic and OpenAI’s top models. However, the high per‑token cost and the 500‑request concurrency cap mean that only workloads that truly need the 49 B active‑parameter capacity will see a net benefit, while high‑throughput services may still prefer the cheaper Flash variant.

## How to think about it
When evaluating V4 Pro for a project, first quantify the token volume and reasoning intensity of your workload. If you need million‑token context windows or complex tool‑calling chains, the reduced compute per token can offset the higher price. For batch‑oriented or latency‑sensitive services, consider the cheaper Flash model or alternative providers. Also, prototype with the non‑thinking or high‑effort modes before moving to V4‑Pro‑Max to control costs.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the advertised benchmark scores will translate directly to your domain without independent testing; the high cost and concurrency limits can quickly become bottlenecks for production workloads.</Callout>
