When a Hacker News post titled “Every Fucking Website” went viral, it exposed a startling truth about modern web startups. The demo shows a single template being regenerated dozens of times, each iteration swapping only copy and colors while preserving the same hero, icon grid, and pricing table. The author notes that roughly 4 million sites on the internet already follow this exact pattern. For developers and product builders, that uniformity threatens brand recall and reduces the impact of even the most polished launch.
<Callout type="takeaway">Most sites today look the same because the tools that power them are optimized for speed, not distinctiveness.</Callout>
## What happened
The “Every Fucking Website” experiment assembles thirty variations of a single design using a predictable set of moves: gradient hero, three feature icons, a fake logo wall, and a three‑tier pricing table with the middle tier highlighted. Each version changes only superficial details like headline copy or color palette, yet the underlying structure remains identical.
The author also points out that there are ~4 million sites on the internet that follow this exact pattern today, illustrating how template‑driven generators have saturated the market with indistinguishable pages.
## Why it matters
When every landing page looks alike, users struggle to form a mental association with any particular brand, eroding recall and reducing conversion potential. Developers may ship faster, but the long‑term cost is a crowded marketplace where differentiation is the true competitive advantage. Teams that rely solely on speed‑first generators risk building products that are forgotten as quickly as they appear.

## How to think about it
Start by defining a clear brand position before you open a code editor. Use AI‑generated components as a rough draft, then deliberately replace the hero image, copy tone, and interaction details with choices that reflect your unique story. Allocate a fixed percentage of your sprint to “distinctiveness work” – for example, a 20 % buffer for custom illustrations, micro‑animations, or unconventional layouts. Finally, test early with real users to see whether the page feels memorable or just another copy.
<Callout type="warning">Do not let an AI choose your brand voice or visual identity without human oversight; the result will likely blend into the sea of generic sites.</Callout>
