A recent peer‑reviewed study examined how common melatonin doses affect cognitive performance after waking. Researchers gave healthy college‑age participants either a placebo, 2 mg, or 5 mg of melatonin the night before a series of morning tests. Both active doses produced slower reaction times and reduced accuracy compared with placebo. The finding is especially relevant for developers and makers who rely on sharp morning focus for coding, debugging, or hardware prototyping. It also highlights the hidden variability in over‑the‑counter melatonin products, particularly in the United States.

<Callout type="takeaway">Even modest melatonin doses can blunt the mental edge needed for early‑day technical work.</Callout>

## What happened
The study, published in Sleep (2023), enrolled healthy young adults (average age early 20s) and administered a single oral dose of either 2 mg, 5 mg melatonin, or a placebo before sleep. The following morning, participants completed a battery of attention, working‑memory, and psychomotor‑speed tasks. Compared with placebo, both melatonin groups showed statistically significant slower reaction times and lower accuracy on the Stroop and digit‑symbol substitution tests.

The authors did not separate the cognitive outcomes by dose in the primary analysis, treating the two active conditions as a single “melatonin” group. Independent reports have documented that many US melatonin supplements contain up to four times the labeled amount, while European products often offer lower strengths such as 0.1 mg, aligning more closely with the 0.3 mg dose recommended by some sleep experts.

## Why it matters
For anyone who schedules coding sprints, design reviews, or hardware debugging in the first hours after waking, a dip in attention can translate into bugs, missed deadlines, or safety oversights. The research suggests that even doses commonly marketed as “low‑dose” (2 mg) are enough to impair the mental sharpness that developers prize. Moreover, the dosage uncertainty in US‑sold melatonin means users may unintentionally consume higher amounts, magnifying the risk.

## How to think about it
Treat melatonin as a sleep‑onset aid, not a performance enhancer. If you need to be at peak mental capacity within two hours of waking, either skip melatonin or choose a formulation no higher than 0.1 mg and verify its content with an independent lab report. Consider timing: taking the supplement at least 30 minutes before bedtime and allowing a full 7‑9 hour sleep window can reduce residual plasma levels by morning. Finally, track your own morning performance metrics (e.g., code compile times, error rates) when you start or stop melatonin to quantify any personal impact.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that “low‑dose” melatonin on the label reflects the actual amount; always check third‑party testing before regular use.</Callout>
