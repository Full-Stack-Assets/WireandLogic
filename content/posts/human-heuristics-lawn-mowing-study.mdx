Researchers at The Pudding built a digital 7 × 7 lawn and invited 30,954 volunteers to trace a mowing path. Participants could see the grid and move a cursor square‑by‑square, trying to cover every tile exactly once. The experiment recorded each move, pause, and overall efficiency compared with the mathematically optimal route. The results show that ordinary people can come remarkably close to the best possible solution, even without formal training in algorithms.

<Callout type="takeaway">Humans naturally generate efficient coverage paths that often rival algorithmic solutions.</Callout>

## What happened
The digital lawn required covering 49 squares. The optimal solution consists of 12 distinct perfect routes, and 16% of the 30,954 players reproduced one of them exactly. A further 52% finished within five moves of the optimum, giving a median efficiency of 91% and generating 14,589 unique paths overall. The study links this task to the classic Coverage Path Planning problem, a variant of the Traveling Salesman Problem, and shows that human participants consistently produce near‑optimal heuristics.

## Why it matters
These findings suggest that human spatial intuition can inform the design of autonomous mowing robots and other coverage‑based systems. By studying the heuristics people employ—focusing attention on decision‑heavy sections and gliding through open areas—engineers can craft lightweight algorithms that approximate optimal routes without exhaustive computation. The results also highlight the value of mixed‑initiative interfaces where humans guide or validate algorithmic plans.

## How to think about it
When designing coverage algorithms, start by identifying “high‑cognitive‑load” zones—tight corners, dead‑ends, or choke points—and allocate more planning resources there. Use simple greedy moves in open spaces, mirroring how participants winged through them. Combine this human‑inspired heuristic with a fallback exact solver for small sub‑problems to ensure overall optimality where needed.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the same performance will hold for much larger or irregular lawns without additional testing.</Callout>
