Meta’s invitation‑only Content Monetisation programme, which disbursed nearly $3 billion to 16.2 million creators in 2025, has been found paying accounts that breach its own policies. An ABC News Verify investigation identified a white nationalist, a far‑right website, an anti‑immigration page and an anti‑vaccine activist as recipients. These creators have posted racist abuse, white‑supremacist rhetoric and medical misinformation while still earning ad revenue. The revelation raises urgent questions for anyone building on Facebook’s ecosystem about brand safety and policy compliance.

<Callout type="takeaway">Meta’s monetisation program is rewarding extremist and misinformation creators, exposing gaps in its policy enforcement.</Callout>

## What happened

The investigation by ABC News Verify and RNZ found that Meta’s Content Monetisation programme paid several Australian Facebook pages that promote extremist or false‑information content. The programme, described by Meta as invitation‑only, distributes advertising revenue to public reels, photos, stories and text posts that meet eligibility criteria.

Identified creators include Hugo Lennon, a far‑right agitator who shouted racist abuse at Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and has neo‑Nazi connections; The Noticer, a website that regularly publishes white‑supremacist and neo‑Nazi articles; a page promoting the anti‑immigration group “March for Australia”; and Monica Smit, founder of the anti‑vaccine group Reignite Democracy Australia, whose page spreads vaccine misinformation and sells “radiation protection” bracelets. Lennon, The Noticer and the March for Australia page joined the programme between September 2025 and December 2025, while Smit has been earning revenue since September 2025 and reportedly as far back as 2017.

Meta disclosed that it paid almost US$3 billion to an estimated 16.2 million monetised accounts in 2025, but the investigation showed that some of those accounts were in direct violation of Meta’s own monetisation rules, which prohibit misleading medical information and restrict certain controversial topics.

## Why it matters

The payments effectively subsidise content that fuels hate, misinformation and public polarization, undermining user trust in the platform. For developers and advertisers who rely on Meta’s APIs and ad products, the presence of extremist publishers in the revenue stream creates brand‑safety risks and may trigger compliance penalties. Moreover, the episode highlights a systemic weakness in Meta’s content‑review automation, suggesting that other undisclosed creators could also be profiting from policy‑breaching material.

## How to think about it

If you build products that ingest Facebook content or place ads on the platform, audit the publisher IDs against independent watchlists and monitor engagement spikes that may indicate rage‑bait. Incorporate automated content‑risk scoring and give users clear opt‑out mechanisms. Diversify revenue channels so that your business does not depend solely on Meta’s monetisation programme, and stay prepared to pivot if policy enforcement changes.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that Meta will automatically remove all policy‑violating creators; proactive monitoring and independent safeguards are essential.</Callout>
