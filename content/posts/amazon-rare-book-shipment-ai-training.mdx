A 404 Media investigation traced a shipment of rare books from a collector to an Amazon warehouse in Las Vegas. The books arrived at a facility identified as VGT3, where employees remove bindings and scan the pages for AI training. The process destroys the physical volumes after digitization. This discovery highlights how large‑scale digitization can threaten cultural artifacts. It raises questions for developers who rely on such data.

<Callout type="takeaway">Amazon is dismantling rare books to feed its AI models, sacrificing physical heritage for digital training data.</Callout>

## What happened
Investigators placed a tracking device in a rare book they suspected would be targeted by an AI‑training operation. The device recorded the book’s journey across the country and confirmed its final stop at an Amazon warehouse in Las Vegas, Nevada.

Warehouse staff, part of a team called VGT3, report that they receive bulk shipments of printed books, cut the bindings off, and scan the pages at high speed. The scanned images are fed into Amazon’s machine‑learning pipelines, and the original books are discarded.

The VGT3 team’s logo features a dinosaur clutching a book, a visual cue that the operation treats the volumes as raw material rather than cultural objects.

## Why it matters
The practice turns unique, often irreplaceable works into disposable data sources, eroding physical heritage while expanding AI capabilities. Developers who train models on such data inherit the ethical and legal baggage of the original acquisition. Moreover, the lack of transparency makes it difficult to assess provenance, which can affect model bias and compliance.

## How to think about it
Developers should audit the provenance of any scanned text they ingest, verify that the source material was obtained with proper rights, and consider alternative datasets that respect cultural preservation. Building pipelines that log source metadata and include ethical review checkpoints can mitigate inadvertent reliance on destroyed works. When possible, partner with libraries or publishers that provide digitized content under clear licensing terms.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that all scanned books are in the public domain; using them without clearance can expose you to copyright liability.</Callout>
