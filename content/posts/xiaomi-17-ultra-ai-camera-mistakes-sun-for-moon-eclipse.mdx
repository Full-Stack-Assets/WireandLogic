On August 12, 2026, a total solar eclipse crossed the Northern Hemisphere, prompting millions to point their phones at the sky. When a user snapped the event with a Xiaomi 17 Ultra, the phone’s built‑in AI enhancer transformed the Sun into a textured lunar disc. The resulting image shows the Sun rendered with craters and a reddish hue, effectively swapping the Sun for a ‘super‑moon’. This glitch highlights the growing influence—and risk—of on‑device AI in photography.

<Callout type="takeaway">Xiaomi’s AI camera mistakenly rendered the Sun as a Moon, exposing the perils of automated image enhancement.</Callout>

## What happened

In a side‑by‑side test of four smartphones capturing the eclipse, the Xiaomi 17 Ultra produced the most striking but erroneous shot. The phone’s AI‑driven astrophotography mode detected a bright orb and applied a lunar texture filter, replacing the Sun’s silhouette with cratered moon‑like details.

The behavior mirrors the 2023 Samsung “Moon” controversy, where zoom‑enhancement algorithms added fictitious lunar surface features. Users posted the altered image on X/Twitter, noting the bright orange Sun now bore the familiar gray‑and‑white relief of the Moon.

## Why it matters

Automated enhancements that rewrite reality can erode trust in mobile photography, especially for events with scientific significance. Developers must balance convenience with transparency, and users need to know when AI has altered the scene. Mis‑labelled images also risk spreading misinformation about astronomical phenomena.

## How to think about it

When building AI‑enhancement pipelines, embed a clear “before/after” preview and let users toggle the effect. Test algorithms on high‑contrast celestial scenes to catch edge cases where the model confuses bright objects. Document any automatic mode that may alter astronomical subjects, and consider disabling it for scientific photography.

<Callout type="warning">Do not rely on AI‑enhanced smartphone photos for scientific or archival purposes without manual verification.</Callout>
