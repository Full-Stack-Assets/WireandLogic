Cloudflare, the backbone that routes roughly a third of all web traffic, has quietly pivoted toward an AI‑first product strategy. The shift has introduced a wave of new features, rapid releases, and a noticeable uptick in service interruptions. At the same time, developers report that the platform’s developer experience feels retrofitted rather than native. For teams that rely on Cloudflare for performance, security, and reliability, these changes could reshape architecture decisions.

<Callout type="takeaway">Cloudflare’s AI‑driven product push is trading reliability for speed, and developers are paying the price.</Callout>

## What happened
Cloudflare continues to dominate the internet infrastructure market, handling about one in three requests worldwide and enjoying an all‑time‑high stock price. Over the past few years the company has launched a series of AI‑powered services and adopted a “dream‑it‑vibe‑ship” mindset, prioritizing rapid feature rollout and social‑media buzz over the meticulous engineering culture that built its core CDN and DNS. The result has been a noticeable increase in outages and a developer experience that feels bolted on, with inconsistent APIs and limited documentation.

## Why it matters
Reliability is the cornerstone of any edge provider; when outages become frequent, latency spikes and security controls can fail, forcing downstream services to implement costly workarounds. The fragmented developer experience also raises the barrier to adoption, meaning teams spend more time debugging integration issues than building features. For startups and enterprises alike, the trade‑off between cutting‑edge AI tools and a stable, well‑documented platform becomes a strategic decision that can affect uptime SLAs and budget allocations.

## How to think about it
Treat Cloudflare’s AI offerings as optional add‑ons rather than the default edge stack. Monitor key performance indicators such as error rates and latency with independent observability tools, and keep a fallback CDN or DNS provider ready for failover. When evaluating new AI features, run them behind feature flags and validate them in a staging environment before production rollout. Finally, engage with Cloudflare’s support and community forums to stay ahead of upcoming changes and to influence product roadmaps.

<Callout type="warning">Do not rely on experimental AI services for mission‑critical traffic until they have a proven stability record.</Callout>
