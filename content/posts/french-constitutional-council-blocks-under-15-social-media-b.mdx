France’s Constitutional Council delivered a decisive blow to the government’s plan to bar anyone under 15 from social‑media platforms. The council ruled that the ban infringed on freedom of expression and privacy, overturning the law passed by parliament in July. The decision forces President Emmanuel Macron to rewrite the legislation before the intended rollout in September. Stakeholders from tech companies to child‑rights groups are watching the outcome closely.

<Callout type="takeaway">The ban was struck down because it was not proportionate, necessary, or adequately protected privacy.</Callout>

## What happened
The nine‑member Constitutional Council found that Article 1 of the legislation “constitutes an infringement that is neither appropriate, necessary, nor proportionate” to the freedom of expression and communication for under‑15s. It also highlighted the lack of legal safeguards around the required proof of age, deeming the measure insufficient to protect privacy.

President Macron immediately asked Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu to draft a new proposal that addresses the council’s concerns. The original bill would have forced platforms to close existing accounts of users under 15 and to verify age for all new accounts starting in September, with a full rollout by early 2027.

## Why it matters
The ruling underscores the tension between protecting children online and preserving fundamental rights. Tech platforms must balance age‑verification tools with privacy obligations, while lawmakers grapple with designing effective, constitutional safeguards. The decision also signals to other EU nations that blanket bans may face legal challenges, shaping the broader European approach to digital child protection.

## How to think about it
Developers should design age‑verification flows that are transparent, data‑minimal, and auditable, avoiding intrusive data collection. Policy teams need to draft provisions that define clear limits and safeguards for any age‑related restrictions, ensuring proportionality and necessity. Companies can engage with regulators early to shape standards that protect privacy while addressing child‑safety concerns.

<Callout type="warning">Do not implement blanket age bans without a clear legal framework; doing so can breach privacy rights and trigger constitutional challenges.</Callout>
