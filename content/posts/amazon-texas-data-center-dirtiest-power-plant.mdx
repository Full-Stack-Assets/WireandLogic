Amazon announced a new data center in Texas that will be powered by a plant identified as the most polluting power source in the United States. The facility will draw electricity from a coal‑fired plant that emits far more CO₂ per megawatt‑hour than the national average. The move has drawn criticism from environmental groups and raises questions about how the company balances rapid growth with its climate pledges. Local officials argue the project will bring jobs and tax revenue, but the environmental trade‑off is stark.

<Callout type="takeaway">Amazon’s Texas data center ties its growth to the dirtiest power source in the country, highlighting a clash between scale and sustainability.</Callout>

## What happened
Amazon’s Texas site is part of a broader expansion that includes multi‑billion‑dollar data center campuses in Missouri and Louisiana, projects that are projected to create hundreds of jobs and generate significant tax revenue for local communities. The Texas facility will source its electricity from an existing coal‑fired power plant that the EPA has flagged as the highest emitter per unit of output in the United States. While Amazon says it is working with the local utility to avoid passing costs to ratepayers, the plant’s emissions profile remains unchanged.

## Why it matters
The decision directly impacts regional carbon footprints, as data centers already account for a growing share of national electricity use. Relying on the dirtiest available plant undermines Amazon’s public sustainability commitments and could trigger regulatory scrutiny or shareholder pressure. Communities may benefit economically, but the health and climate costs of higher pollution levels could offset those gains.

## How to think about it
When evaluating new data‑center projects, weigh the economic incentives against the energy mix of the local grid. Prioritize sites that can source power from renewable or low‑carbon generators, and factor in the cost of carbon offsets or on‑site clean‑energy investments. Build a framework that includes lifecycle emissions, community impact, and compliance risk so that growth does not come at the expense of sustainability.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that Amazon will automatically offset the emissions from this plant; explicit offset purchases or renewable contracts are required to mitigate the impact.</Callout>
