Recent releases from leading AI labs show a striking shift: models are being engineered to excel at reasoning while deliberately shedding factual knowledge. Benchmarks like AIME 2026 report record‑high scores for math and code tasks, with GLM‑5.2 reaching 99.2 % using only about 40 billion active parameters per token. At the same time, factual recall on SimpleQA has stalled, with the best model, Gemini 2.5 Pro, answering just 53 % of questions. This trade‑off matters because it reshapes what developers can trust models to do out of the box.
<Callout type="takeaway">Models are getting better at reasoning at the expense of factual accuracy, and the shift is intentional.</Callout>
## What happened
Reasoning scores keep climbing while per‑token compute drops. GLM‑5.2 scored 99.2 % on the AIME 2026 benchmark with roughly 40 billion active parameters per token, Qwen3.5 hit 91.3 % with 17 billion, and DeepSeek V4‑Flash runs 13 billion active. By contrast, GPT‑4 in 2023 reportedly used around 280 billion active parameters and could barely solve an AIME problem, highlighting the efficiency gains of newer models.
On factual recall the picture is opposite. SimpleQA, a benchmark that forbids tool use, lists Gemini 2.5 Pro as the leader at only 53 % accuracy, and smaller models score far lower. Artificial Analysis reports hallucination rates of 80‑82 % for Qwen3.5 models on knowledge tests, meaning most unanswered facts are fabricated. Research on knowledge capacity estimates roughly two bits of factual knowledge per parameter, so shedding knowledge saves a substantial portion of the model’s weight budget.
## Why it matters
Developers who rely on language models for data‑driven tasks—code generation, documentation, or decision support—must recognize that high reasoning scores no longer guarantee reliable factual output. Hallucinations can propagate misinformation, increase debugging time, and erode user trust. At the same time, the efficiency gains enable deployment on cheaper hardware, opening up new use‑cases for edge devices and low‑cost services.

## How to think about it
Treat reasoning‑focused models as specialized tools rather than general‑purpose knowledge bases. For tasks that require factual accuracy, layer a retrieval or verification component—such as a search API or a smaller knowledge‑tuned model—before trusting the output. When evaluating new models, look beyond single‑metric leaderboards and examine both reasoning and factual benchmarks side by side. Finally, consider the cost‑benefit trade‑off: if your workload is math‑heavy and can tolerate occasional fact checks, a smaller, reasoning‑optimized model may be the most efficient choice.
<Callout type="warning">Do not deploy these models as sole sources of truth for critical factual information without an external verification step.</Callout>
